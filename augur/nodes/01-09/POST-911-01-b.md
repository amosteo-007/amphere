## (b) Volitional Actor Responses

The following decisions were made by named actors in direct response to the September 11 attacks. Each represents a choice that could have gone differently — a different actor, a different decision, a different outcome.

| Actor | Decision | Date | Counterfactual Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FAA Administrator Jane Garvey** | Chose to order all inbound flights to land immediately; later ordered all civilian aircraft in U.S. airspace to land — first-ever nationwide grounding | ~9:55 AM ET / ~12:15 PM ET, Sep 11 | **High** — Without this decision, dozens of aircraft would have remained airborne over a country in active terrorist emergency; mid-air collisions or further attacks via hijacked planes would have been far more likely |
| **President George W. Bush** | Chose to declare the attacks "a terrible tragedy" aboard Air Force One; later addressed the nation from the White House at 8:30 PM ET | Morning/afternoon, Sep 11 | **Medium** — Leadership framing was consequential, but multiple viable communication alternatives existed |
| **President George W. Bush** | Chose to sign emergency congressional supplemental appropriations ($40 billion initial package, H.R. 2888) | Sep 14 | **Low** — Broad bipartisan consensus existed; passage was virtually certain regardless |
| **President George W. Bush** | Chose to authorize NSA wiretapping without FISA court approval (initial authorization, later retroactively codified) | Sep 18 | **Medium** — A major surveillance policy shift; plausible without 9/11 but timing and scope were directly triggered by the attack |
| **President George W. Bush** | Chose to address joint session of Congress and declare "either you are with us, or you are with the terrorists" — framing the War on Terror | Sep 20 | **Medium** — Rhetorical framing shaped global alliances; alternatives existed but this framing dominated |
| **President George W. Bush** | Chose to sign executive orders: (1) freezing terrorist-linked assets, (2) establishing military tribunals, (3) authorizing CIA covert action in Afghanistan | Sep 24–25 | **High** — War cabinet decisions on Afghanistan intervention directly led to the October 2001 invasion; without these choices, al-Qaeda leadership would likely have remained sheltered |
| **Fed Chair Alan Greenspan** | Chose to order the Federal Reserve to provide "adequate liquidity" to the banking system; coordinated with ECB and other central banks to limit dollar trading disruptions | Sep 11 | **High** — Without immediate liquidity injection, interbank markets faced potential seizure; systemic banking crisis was averted |
| **Fed Chair Alan Greenspan** | Chose to cut the federal funds rate by 50 basis points to 3.0% (emergency inter-meeting action) and lower the discount rate by 50 bps | Sep 17 | **Medium** — Rate cut signaled confidence and cushioned the economic shock; market stabilization was probable without it but would have been slower and more volatile |
| **Congress (House and Senate)** | Chose to pass H.R. 2888 — Emergency Supplemental Appropriations Act ($40 billion) | Sep 14 | **Low** — Near-unanimous bipartisan vote; virtually certain to pass |
| **Congress** | Chose to pass the Air Transportation Safety and System Stabilization Act — direct compensation to airlines and victims | Sep 21 | **Medium** — Without this legislation, airline industry bankruptcy risk was high |
| **Congress** | Chose to approve $15 billion airline bailout ($5B in direct grants + $10B in loan guarantees) | Sep 22 | **Medium** — Without the bailout, several major carriers likely would have filed for Chapter 11 within weeks; industry consolidation would have been severe |
| **Major Airlines (American, United, Delta)** | Chose to ground all operations entirely (Sep 11–13); later voluntarily reduced schedules by 20–30% as passenger confidence collapsed | Sep 11–25 | **High** (grounding) — FAA order was mandatory; airline volition was suspended during shutdown. **Medium** (schedule cuts) — Even with partial passenger return, demand collapse was real; cuts would have been deeper/longer without the bailout |
| **NYC Mayor Rudy Giuliani** | Chose to declare a state of emergency, establish field hospitals, and coordinate FDNY/PD/NYC response from Ground Zero | Sep 11 | **High** — Leadership during the crisis prevented secondary panic; Giuliani's decisions shaped immediate rescue and recovery operations |
| **NYC Mayor Rudy Giuliani** | Chose to hold daily briefings, form the Family Assistance Center, and manage mass casualty response | Sep 12–25 | **Medium** — Alternatives existed but Giuliani's pre-9/11 emergency management reputation shaped institutional response |
| **SecDef / DoD** | Chose to order all U.S. military bases to Condition Charlie and initiate continuity of government protocols | Sep 11 (afternoon) | **High** — These decisions were essential to maintaining governmental function during the attack and its aftermath |
| **SecDef / DoD** | Chose to authorize deployment of Civil Support Teams and activate 56,000 Air National Guard members for air defense | Sep 14 | **High** — Without this, domestic air defense would have been severely degraded during the critical post-attack period |
| **Secretary of State Colin Powell** | Chose to coordinate NATO Article 5 invocation (NATO's first-ever triggering) and build UN Security Council Resolution 1368 consensus | Sep 12–20 | **Medium** — International legitimacy coalition shaped subsequent Afghanistan intervention; without this diplomatic groundwork, allied support would have been weaker |

**Confidence on actor decisions:** **0.73** — Decisions are well-documented through primary sources (9/11 Commission Report, congressional records, Fed archives, official government statements). Minor uncertainties exist around exact deliberation sequences and internal discussions that were not fully public. Multiple independent sources corroborate each major decision listed.

**Confidence on counterfactual assessments:** **0.58** — Counterfactual reasoning is inherently speculative. While decisions are documented, the certainty with which we can claim "a different decision would have produced a different outcome" varies. High-dependency assessments (FAA grounding, Fed liquidity, Giuliani's crisis leadership) rest on reasonably strong inference. Low-dependency assessments (congressional vote) are near-certain. Medium-dependency items carry the most uncertainty. All counterfactual scores should be reviewed by a domain expert before final calibration.

**Note on omitted actors:** Several additional decisions were made in the T-14 to T+25 window (e.g., SEC emergency market closures, individual airline refund policies, state-level emergency declarations). The table above captures the highest-impact, best-documented volitional choices. Secondary actors and minor decisions are noted here for completeness but were not individually tabled due to source granularity constraints.
